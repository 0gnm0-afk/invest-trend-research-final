# Descriptive distribution supplement

This appendix was added **after pooled aggregation** highlighted a large mean onset-to-confirmation rise among rare US TRIX-zero early successes. It is an explicitly post-aggregation descriptive supplement. It introduces no new hypothesis, signal, parameter, eligibility rule, outcome horizon, matching bin or preferred time window. No observations are trimmed or winsorized.

`path-distributions.csv` covers the fixed 26 KR / 25 US focal conditions. Every condition has separate rows for all eligible path signals and the retrospectively identified early-confirmation successes, at 5, 21 and 63 bars. Each horizon reports terminal return, favorable excursion and adverse depth. The all-signal population has no L1 filter; the success population is diagnostic and must not be substituted for it.

The reference is `open[t+1]`. Terminal return is `close[t+h]/open[t+1]−1`. Favorable excursion is `max(0,max(high[t+1:t+h])/open[t+1]−1)`; adverse depth is `max(0,1−min(low[t+1:t+h])/open[t+1])`, with both endpoint bars included. The CSV reports count, mean, minimum, q05, q10, q25, median, q75, q90, q95, maximum and probability that the reported outcome exceeds zero. For terminal return this last column is the positive terminal-return frequency. For favorable/adverse excursions it merely indicates that the corresponding excursion occurred; it is not a win rate.

`first-confirmation-distributions.csv` counts **one first successful early signal per eligible episode**. It reuses the classifier's 65-prior / 126-subsequent contiguous-bar guard, completed rise of at least 10 positive edges, fully observable early ages 0–5 and feature availability on every early-window date. The measured amount is `open[first_signal+1]/close[retrospective_onset]−1`. It is how far the raw price had already moved before the first successful confirmation, not a subsequent holding return. Its denominator differs from all early-hit alert dates, because multiple alerts can occur within one episode.

`first-confirmation-extremes.csv` identifies the three largest observed first-confirmation rises per fixed condition, with symbols, dates, onset closes and reference opens. This permits inspection of mean sensitivity and exceptional observations. These records are diagnostic traces, not examples selected to demonstrate typical performance. The separate representative-case figures use the median-return selection rule.

Computation reads each stock once, writes small float32 binary spools and calculates each empirical quantile directly with NumPy's linear interpolation. Quantiles are exact for the stored float32 values, with no sketching or quantile approximation. Means are also retained from the original float64 price calculation for comparison. The binary representation can introduce minute rounding differences; verification records their observed magnitude.

Every per-stock path count and sum is checked against the existing all-signal/early-hit sufficient statistics; terminal positive frequencies must agree. Every per-stock first-confirmation count and sum is checked against the original episode statistics, then pooled counts and means are reconciled against the primary metrics. `verification-distributions.json` records completion, numerical differences and output row counts. These distribution rows have **no new inferential confidence intervals**; the predeclared dependent-resampling intervals remain in the main analysis.

A large difference between mean and median makes the mean's sensitivity visible. It does not by itself show that an observation is erroneous, nor authorize removing it. Unusual prices remain subject to the inherited provider, corporate-action adjustment and historical coverage limitations. Path maxima and favorable excursions remain unavailable as automatically realizable trading profits.

After all distribution checks passed, the153temporary binary spool files (493,527,612bytes) were individually removed to recover disk space. Their exact resolved paths, sizes and SHA-256hashes were saved **before** removal in `distributions-spool-cleanup.json`. Every CSV, extreme trace, completed-spool receipt, verification receipt and immutable source/path/evaluation artifact was retained. No other study's files were touched.

The spools are reproducible with `python scripts/research/uptrend_longhistory/distributions.py --rebuild-spool`; missing derived feature caches can also be rebuilt from the preserved source prices/fits. `distributions-replay-amendment.json` distinguishes the executed computation hash from the subsequent replay-only CLI amendment. The numerical formulas were unchanged by that amendment.
